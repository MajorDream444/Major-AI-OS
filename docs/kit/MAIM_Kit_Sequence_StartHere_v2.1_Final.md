## EMAIL 4 — DAY 5
**Subject:** C is for Context. This is why most AI outputs disappoint.
**Preview:** The problem isn't the tool. It's the input.

---

Hey {{ subscriber.first_name | default: "friend" }},

C is for Context.

And this one explains everything.

Most people who try AI and come away disappointed made the same mistake: they gave the tool a task with no context. They wrote: *"Write me a post about my business."*

And they got something that sounded like a LinkedIn post written by someone who has never met them, doesn't know their audience, doesn't understand their culture, and has no idea what makes their work matter.

Which is exactly what happened — because that's exactly what they asked for.

AI is not a mind reader. It is a mirror. It reflects back what you put in. Give it nothing, get nothing. Give it everything — who you are, who you're talking to, what you sound like, what you're trying to do — and the output changes completely.

**Clear input. Clear output.**

For Caribbean builders specifically, this matters even more. When you don't explicitly tell the tool who you are, where you're building from, and who you're building for, the output defaults to the most generic version of your request — stripped of culture, community, and specificity. Your voice disappears. The grounding disappears. And the result feels off in a way you can't quite name but immediately feel.

The tool isn't working against you. It's just working with nothing. Context is the fix.

**YOUR MAJOR MOVE — C · CONTEXT:**
The next time you use any AI tool, start your prompt with this:

*"I am a [your role/identity] building for [your community/audience]. My voice is [warm/direct/culturally grounded/etc]. My work is about [what you actually do]. With that context in mind — [your actual request]."*

That one addition changes the quality of everything that follows.

One letter. One lesson. One action.

## EMAIL 6 — DAY 10
**Subject:** E is for Experiment. The builders who win do this one thing differently.
**Preview:** Stop trying to get it right. Start trying to find out what works.

---

Hey {{ subscriber.first_name | default: "friend" }},

E is for Experiment.

And this is the letter that separates the people who use AI once and give up from the people who build real systems with it.

The difference is mindset.

The person who gives up is trying to *get it right the first time*. They write one prompt, get an output that isn't perfect, and conclude that AI doesn't work for them. They needed a win and got a draft, so they stopped.

The person who builds with it is *trying to find out what works*. They write one prompt, look at the output, change one variable, look at the new output, and keep iterating. They're not looking for a result. They're building a process.

The builders who win treat AI like a lab, not a vending machine.

Here's the thing about experimenting that most people miss: **you learn more from a bad output than a good one**. A bad output tells you exactly what information was missing from your input. It's the clearest feedback you'll ever get on your own thinking.

So the next time AI gives you something that misses the mark — instead of getting frustrated, get curious. Ask: *what didn't I tell it? What did I assume it knew that it didn't?*

That question will make you better faster than any tutorial ever will.

**YOUR MAJOR MOVE — E · EXPERIMENT:**
Take something you've already asked AI to do this week — a post, an email, a plan, anything. Run the output through this filter:

*"What's the one thing I didn't tell the tool that would have changed this output?"*

Add that missing piece to your original prompt. Run it again. Compare the two outputs.

That comparison is your experiment. That learning is your edge.

One letter. One lesson. One action.

— Major Dream
